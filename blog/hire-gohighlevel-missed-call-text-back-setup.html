What Does the Built-In Missed Call Text Back Do?

It sends one SMS to the caller after an inbound call goes unanswered. You find it in the sub-account under Settings, then Phone System, then Voice, then Voicemail & Missed Call Text Back. Tick the box to enable the feature, click Customize, and write your message in the Enter message field. There is a field below the editor where you can send yourself a test before you save.

Two defaults shape the behavior. The incoming call timeout slider decides when a call even counts as missed, and HighLevel recommends 10 to 20 seconds. Answered calls never trigger a text back. Merge fields only fill in when the system already knows the contact, so a first-time caller sees your message exactly as written. HighLevel also picks the sending number: the number the caller dialed first, then a number you have already messaged that contact from, then your default outbound number.

Repeat callers get a text every time

HighLevel says so in its own documentation: the feature triggers an SMS for every missed call, even when the same caller tries several times within a brief timeframe. Call, hang up, call again, and that caller gets a second text. A third attempt gets a third, and your business starts to look like a malfunctioning robot.

Their recommended fix is to stop treating the toggle as the whole feature: customize the existing missed call text-back recipe in Workflows, then add a wait step of around 20 minutes or tags such as first call and second call so a repeat attempt routes somewhere different. That guidance is also the first honest test of whoever you hire. Anyone who says the checkbox covers it has not read the page.

Where Does the Default Setup Stop and a Real Build Start?

The toggle answers a call. A build decides what happens to the lead. Six things sit in the gap.

  • Pipeline entry. A missed caller never lands in a stage you can track or assign, because the default logs the call and moves no card. A build creates the opportunity with the source attached alongside it.
  • Owner notification. Nobody notices a text a machine sent. A build notifies the assigned user or a round robin group and creates a callback task with a due time.
  • After-hours cover. The text reads the same at 2pm and at 2am. A build branches on when the call landed and sets the callback for the next business window.
  • AI or voicemail fallback. A build can route the number to a Voice AI agent that answers when nobody picks up, or keep voicemail in the chain with a greeting that says what happens next. Missed Call WhatsApp Back is a separate feature for WhatsApp-first leads.
  • Booking link. HighLevel's setup guides suggest putting your calendar link in the message itself. A build points that link at a calendar with open availability, plus confirmation, reminder, and no-show steps.
  • Attribution. Call tracking number pools, plus the In Number Pool and In Phone Number filters on the call trigger, let a build tag each lead with its source.

None of it needs exotic tooling. The difference between a text message and a system that owns the lead is what runs after the text.

What Build Order Does a Professional Follow?

Order matters because each layer depends on the one beneath it.

  1. Numbers and routing first. Confirm which numbers receive lead calls, whether forwarding is involved, and the ring timeout.
  2. Messages next. Write the standard text, the after-hours version, and the second-attempt version, then check each one for length and merged values.
  3. Suppression rules third. Add the wait step or tag filters so a repeat caller gets one useful follow-up instead of five copies. Check the workflow's re-entry setting while you are there, because repeat enrollment follows that control, not your intent.
  4. Pipeline and ownership after that. Create the opportunity, set the stage, assign an owner, then add the notification and the callback task.
  5. Booking links next. Only point the message at a calendar you have already connected and tested, with availability that matches how you work.
  6. Tags and reporting last, once the paths are stable enough to measure.

Suppression before pipeline entry stops you from creating five opportunities for one caller. Booking links before calendar checks push people toward a diary that is not open.

How Do You QA a Missed Call Text Back Build With Test Calls?

You cannot QA this from a settings screen. You have to call yourself, from numbers that are not in the CRM.

  • Call from an unknown number, let it ring past the timeout, and confirm one text arrives with the wording you saved.
  • Call twice more within a few minutes. Count the messages. Three texts mean your suppression step or re-entry setting is wrong.
  • Call after hours and confirm the after-hours message fires, not the daytime one.
  • Reply to the text with a real question and confirm the conversation continues in Conversations.
  • Open the contact and confirm an owner, the right pipeline stage, and a callback task with a due time.
  • Click the booking link on a phone. Book, then confirm the appointment lands on the right calendar with the right owner.
  • Test a caller with DND set, since HighLevel respects communication preferences and that contact should not receive the text.
  • Call a tracking number tied to one campaign and confirm the source tag lands on the record.

Log every result with a timestamp. When a test fails, fix the setting behind it and rerun the whole path, not just the step that broke.

What Should You Ask Before You Pay Someone to Set This Up?

These questions separate a builder from someone who knows where the checkbox lives.

  1. How do you stop repeat texts when someone calls three times? You want to hear about a wait step or tag filters. "It does not do that" ends the conversation.
  2. What happens to the lead after the text? The answer should include a pipeline stage, an owner, and a callback task.
  3. What changes after hours? A different message and a next-day callback, not the same text at 3am.
  4. What happens when a caller replies and never books? There should be a follow-up path behind it.
  5. How will I know which number the lead called? Expect number pools and source tags.
  6. How do you prove it works before handoff? Expect test calls from outside numbers and a written result.
  7. Who owns it after handoff? A snapshot, documented custom values, and a short walkthrough your team can run.

The wider list, covering discovery, access, and handoff, sits in the questions to ask before you hire a GoHighLevel expert.

What Does Good Look Like 30 Days In?

Thirty days in, you should be able to watch these behaviors without asking anyone.

  • Every missed call has a text in the thread, and the text matches the hour it was sent.
  • The caller who rang three times got one reply, not four.
  • Every missed caller has an owner and a stage, so the day's misses show up in the pipeline.
  • Callback tasks get closed, reassigned, or escalated, and you can see who did what.
  • Bookings from missed calls appear on the calendar with the source attached.
  • Nobody asks "did anyone call this person back?" because the answer is in the record.

HighLevel's own messaging on this feature notes that most people do not leave voicemails and rarely call back, which is the argument for the automatic reply. One text buys a second chance. Capture, owner, and booking path decide whether it turns into a call.

FAQ

Does GoHighLevel missed call text back send a text on every missed call?

Yes. HighLevel documents that the feature triggers an SMS notification for every missed call, even when the same caller tries several times in a short window. To avoid repeat messages, HighLevel recommends customizing the missed call text-back recipe in Workflows and adding a wait step of roughly 20 minutes, or using tags such as first call and second call to route repeat attempts differently.

Where do I turn on missed call text back in GoHighLevel?

In the sub-account, open Settings, then Phone System, then Voice, then Voicemail & Missed Call Text Back, and select Missed Call Text Back. Enable the feature, click Customize to write the message in the Enter message field, send yourself a test message, then save. Depending on your account version, the same toggle can also appear through a phone number's Advanced Settings or in the location's Business Profile settings.

Does the built-in feature create a pipeline opportunity or notify my team?

No. The built-in feature sends an SMS acknowledgement to the caller. Creating an opportunity, assigning an owner, notifying a user, and creating a callback task all come from workflow automation you build on top of it. Those are the parts a switched-on toggle does not cover.

How do I test missed call text back before going live?

Call the number from a phone that is not in your CRM and let it ring past the incoming call timeout, which HighLevel recommends setting at 10 to 20 seconds. Confirm one text arrives with the saved wording, then call twice more to check repeat-call suppression. Test after hours, reply to the text, click the booking link on a mobile device, and confirm the contact lands in the right pipeline stage with an owner and a callback task.
