Most agency owners do not hire the wrong expert because they pay too little. They hire wrong because they ask surface questions like "Can you build funnels?" Every builder says yes. Then the pipelines leak, the automations fire twice, the calendar shows the wrong timezone, and no one knows how to fix it.

Use these 7 questions before you pay. Listen for specifics. Vague answers predict vague builds.

1. How many GoHighLevel systems have you taken from snapshot to launch?

You want a builder who ships complete systems, not someone who tweaks one workflow and calls it a build. Snapshot to launch means pipelines, stages, custom fields, calendars, forms, funnels, workflows, triggers, tags, users, domains, and QA.

What a good answer sounds like

"I have shipped 100+ systems from snapshot to launch. Here is my checklist: account settings, pipelines, calendars, workflows, QA test contacts, then handoff video. I support 400 to 700+ subaccounts across agencies, so I know where launches break."

What a bad answer sounds like

"I have worked in GoHighLevel for a while. I can figure out your setup." No number. No process. No proof of launches.

Red flag: They show screenshots but cannot walk you through a launch checklist in 2 minutes.

2. Can you show work for clients in my model?

A home services pipeline differs from a coaching funnel or an MSP nurture. Ask for relevant builds. Name the client type, the pipeline stages, and the automation logic.

What a good answer sounds like

"For LeadJuice I handled lead systems at scale. For Seven Figure MSP I built nurture specific to MSP sales cycles. For StandOut Funnels I focused on funnel structure and follow up. Tell me your offer and I will show the closest match."

What a bad answer sounds like

"I built many funnels for many niches." No names. No specifics. No logic explained.

Red flag: They refuse a live screen share of a past build or test account. Experienced builders explain logic fast.

3. What is your process for pipelines, automations, and QA?

This question filters hobbyists. A pro follows steps in order and tests before handoff. Amateurs build workflows first, then patch pipelines to fit.

What a good answer sounds like

"First I map stages to your sales process. Then I build custom fields and calendars. Then workflows for speed to lead, nurture, no show, and review. Then I run test contacts through every path and check tags, tasks, SMS limits, and calendar holds. Only then I hand off."

What a bad answer sounds like

"I will set up automations as we go and we can test live with real leads." That means your leads become the QA.

For detail on the follow up structure I recommend, read GoHighLevel automations that hold pipeline value and speed to lead setup.

Red flag: No QA step. No test contact. No checklist.

4. How do you handle subaccounts, snapshots, and permissions at scale?

Agencies break when snapshot pushes overwrite custom values or when every user gets admin access. If you run multiple locations or clients, you need clean architecture.

What a good answer sounds like

"I have supported 400 to 700+ subaccounts, so I keep snapshots modular. Core snapshot holds pipelines and workflows. Location data stays in custom values. I set roles, limit API and workflow delete rights, and document what to change per subaccount versus what to leave locked."

What a bad answer sounds like

"I will clone the main account for each client." That clones the mess too.

Red flag: They have never managed more than a few subaccounts and cannot explain snapshot hygiene.

5. What do you need from me, and what happens if I go quiet?

Builds stall because assets sit in inboxes. Domains, DNS, email sending, phone numbers, ad accounts, logos, offer details. A pro lists blockers on day one and keeps momentum without you.

What a good answer sounds like

"I send a one page asset list after our scope call: domain registrar login or DNS access, sending email, LC phone, calendar availability, offer and pipeline stages. If you go quiet, I build with placeholders, record gaps in a tracker, and hold QA until you approve."

What a bad answer sounds like

"Just give me login and I will start." No list. No tracker. Then silence for two weeks.

Red flag: No onboarding doc. No timeline tied to asset delivery.

6. What exactly do I own, and how do you hand it off?

You should own everything inside your account. Snapshot, workflows, funnels, calendars, custom fields, forms, docs. Handoff should include training so your team can run it.

What a good answer sounds like

"You own 100 percent. I build inside your account. At handoff you get a walkthrough video, SOPs for daily tasks, and a 30 minute training call. My Extendly students get the same clarity: 70+ coached, so I teach in plain steps."

What a bad answer sounds like

"I will send you the login when done." No video. No SOPs. No training. You inherit a black box.

If you plan to delegate daily ops after launch, read when to hire a GoHighLevel VA.

Red flag: They build in their own agency account and charge you to keep access.

7. What does support look like after launch?

Every system needs a short hypercare window. Workflows hit SMS limits. Calendars need buffer tweaks. Users add tags that break filters. Ask who fixes what, and how fast.

What a good answer sounds like

"I include a QA window after launch. You send Loom or tickets, I fix build errors. I log change requests separately with fixed pricing. I tell you what is covered and what counts as new scope."

What a bad answer sounds like

"Just message me anytime, I am always available." No window. No ticketing. No scope boundary. That breaks in week two.

Red flag: Lifetime free support promised with no process. It signals no boundaries and slow replies later.

Red flags that should stop the hire

  • Cannot name systems shipped or subaccounts supported with specifics.
  • No snapshot to launch checklist, no QA with test contacts.
  • Builds in their account, not yours, and holds access hostage.
  • No asset list, no timeline, no handoff video or SOPs.
  • Promises results they cannot control, like guaranteed ROAS or lead volume.

I stick to what I control: clean architecture, tested automations, clear handoff, and training your team can follow.

FAQ

How much does it cost to hire a GoHighLevel expert?

Cost depends on scope. A single snapshot install costs less than a full snapshot to launch build with pipelines, calendars, automations, QA, and training. Ask for fixed deliverables, timeline, and exclusions. Book a scope call to get a clear quote.

How long does a GoHighLevel setup take?

A clean build takes days, not months, when scope and assets are clear. Delays come from missing DNS, phone, email sending, or unclear offers. I list needs on day one and hold QA until you approve.

Should I hire a GoHighLevel expert or a VA?

Hire an expert to architect and build, then a VA to run it. The expert sets pipelines, automations, and QA. The VA handles inbox, tagging, and bookings. See hire a GoHighLevel VA for the split.

What do I own after the build?

You own everything in your account, plus walkthrough video and SOPs. No lock in. Your team can run it without me.
