How to hire a GoHighLevel VA that actually works.

By Joe Asis — GoHighLevel Systems Architect — 5 min read

Most agency owners hire a GoHighLevel VA backwards. They hand over admin access to a live account, drop a few Loom links in Slack, and hope. Two weeks later the pipelines are a mess, tags multiplied like rabbits, and the VA waits on replies instead of shipping.

I have trained 70+ students and VAs through daily live lessons, run a community where VAs access my resources and recorded breakdowns, and stayed alongside placed VAs as long as they remain with the client. The hires that succeed share one thing: the setup around them.

1. Scope one outcome before you post the job

Bad job post: need GHL VA for everything. Good job post: own calendar management, inbox triage, and weekly pipeline cleanup for two subaccounts. A VA succeeds when one outcome defines done. Name the subaccounts, name the workflows they may touch, and name what stays with you: strategy, offers, client calls.

2. Give them a subaccount built for handoff, not your live mess

Never onboard a VA into the account you built by trial and error. Clone a clean snapshot, verify domains and calendars, confirm user roles limit them to what they need, and document the pipeline stages in plain language. My rule from 100+ delivered systems: if a new hire cannot describe what each pipeline stage means after reading one page, the system is not ready for them.

3. Replace Looms with a QA checklist

Looms explain; checklists enforce. Every repeating task gets steps with a definition of done: calendar checked against double-booking, tags use the UTM glossary, opportunities moved the same day they reply. The VA self-checks before marking anything complete, and you spot-check instead of re-doing.

4. Keep them inside a training loop

The VAs who last all keep learning after hiring. Daily or weekly troubleshooting beats a one-time onboarding call. My community works this way: placed VAs keep access to resources, recorded tips, and hack breakdowns, and I stay available while they hold the seat. Attendance at live huddles stays optional; results stay mandatory.

5. Pay for output, review output weekly

Decide the rate question before interviewing, not during. Then review one metric per week: speed-to-lead under five minutes, inbox at zero daily, pipelines current every Friday. One metric, reviewed weekly, beats ten metrics reviewed never.

Hiring FAQ

How much does a GoHighLevel VA cost?

Rates vary by skill: task VAs cost less than builder-level VAs who own workflows and integrations. Price the outcome, then hire to it.

Should my VA get admin access?

No. Scoped user roles. Admin stays with you until the VA has months of clean QA sheets.

How fast should a new VA become productive?

With a scoped subaccount, SOPs, and a QA checklist: productive in week one, independent by week four.
